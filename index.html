Prepay the trip now, or pay when you get there?

Prepaying locks in today's exchange rate and earns card points now. Waiting keeps your cash earning interest — and leaves you exposed to whatever the yen does between now and your trip. This puts both on the same footing and tells you how far the rate has to move before the answer flips.

What the math can't price

01

Refundability. A prepaid rate is usually non-refundable or penalty-heavy. If the trip could change, that flexibility is worth real money the calculator above doesn't see.

02

Chargeback leverage. Paying at the counter in March keeps your card dispute rights close to the stay. Prepaying seven months out means a dispute window that may close before you travel.

03

Prepay discounts. Many hotels shave 5–15% off for prepaid, non-refundable bookings. If yours does, that discount usually swamps everything modeled here — enter the discounted amount and compare.

04

Point value. The rewards fields assume points are worth about a cent each. If you redeem for outsized value — or if the spend triggers a sign-up bonus — prepaying is worth more than shown.

05

The odds are a model, not a forecast. They assume the rate wanders randomly from today's level with no drift. Nobody knows where USD/JPY lands in March.

Break-even rate R* = R₀ × (1+fee_late)(1−rew_late) ÷ [(1+fee_pre)(1−rew_pre) × (1+r)^t], where R is yen per dollar, r is your after-tax yield and t is years until the payment date. Everything is compared in March dollars. Not financial advice.
