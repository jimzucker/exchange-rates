Should you pay for your trip now, or when you get there?

Hotels and tours often let you prepay. Prepaying locks in today's exchange rate and earns your card points now — but your money stops earning interest, and you're betting the currency won't move in your favour. Four questions below, and this works out which side you're on and by how much.

What the math can't price

01

Refundability. A prepaid rate is usually non-refundable or penalty-heavy. If the trip could change, that flexibility is worth real money nothing above can see.

02

Chargeback leverage. Paying on arrival keeps your card dispute rights close to the stay. Prepaying many months out can mean the dispute window closes before you travel.

03

Prepay discounts. Many hotels shave 5–15% off for prepaid, non-refundable bookings. If yours does, that discount usually swamps everything here — put the discounted amount in and compare.

04

Point value. The rewards fields assume points are worth about a cent each. Redeem for outsized value, or trigger a sign-up bonus, and prepaying is worth more than shown.

05

The odds are a tally, not a forecast. Where history allows, they count how often this pair actually moved far enough over every overlapping window since 1999 — no bell curve assumed, so real fat tails survive. Those windows overlap and the past is not the future; treat it as a frequency, not a probability.

06

The forward is a price, not a prediction. It is fixed by arbitrage from the two countries' interest rates, which is why it always sits between today's rate and break-even — the gap is exactly the interest the other currency earns. It tells you what the market will guarantee you today, not what will happen. Real forwards also use money-market rates rather than the policy rates here, and some currencies carry a basis this misses.

R* = R₀ × (1+fee_late)(1−rew_late) ÷ [(1+fee_pre)(1−rew_pre) × (1+r)^t] — where R is local currency per unit of home currency, r is your after-tax yield and t is years until the payment date. Both sides are compared in future money. Prepaying wins when the rate on the day is below R*.
